Adoption of Sustainability-Related Technology and Practice in Denim Manufacturing

A dedicated research presentation of the 2025 ITAA Proceedings study by Md Mehedi Hassan, Md Arif Iqbal and Md Mazed Islam, using the Technology–Organization–Environment (TOE) framework.

01 · Research question

What is the study trying to understand?

The paper identifies an empirical gap in understanding how sustainability-related technologies and practices are adopted within specific large-scale denim manufacturing settings.

How are sustainability-related technologies and practices adopted in a large denim fabric factory, and what Technology, Organization and Environment factors shape that adoption?
Study objective.

The paper's stated objective is to systematically document, analyze and interpret adoption of sustainability-related technologies and practices including reductions in chemical use, improved water and energy management, and incorporation of recycled materials in denim manufacturing. This page presents that objective as a research question for clarity; it is not a verbatim question from the paper.
02 · Theoretical framework

The TOE lens

The study organizes themes generated from the data into three dimensions of the Technology–Organization–Environment framework.

T

Technology

What technical options and process changes are being adopted?

  • Liquid dyes
  • Reduced alkali consumption
  • Reuse of sodium hydroxide solution
  • STF in denim finishing
O

Organization

What internal organizational changes accompany sustainability adoption?

  • Occupational safety measures
  • Internal compliance
  • Employee retention
  • Buyer engagement
E

Environment

What external and environmental pressures or conditions influence adoption?

  • Recycled yarn use
  • Water reuse
  • Reduced groundwater lifting
  • Buyer pressure & competitiveness
03 · Methodology

How the evidence was built.

An in-depth qualitative case study designed around longitudinal observation and triangulation.

Research design

The study used an in-depth observational qualitative case study in a large denim fabric factory in Bangladesh. Data collection covered January 2023 to December 2024.

Primary methodDirect observation & field notes
Primary methodSemi-structured interviews
EvidenceInternal sustainability reports
EvidenceTechnical specifications

Analysis & reliability

Data analysis followed thematic-analysis guidelines. Initial coding categorized descriptive codes according to TOE dimensions.

Triangulation across observations, interviews and documents was used to enhance accuracy and validity, reduce researcher bias and strengthen reliability.

The paper also notes that an executive co-author provided access to factory documentation and contextual understanding of organizational decision-making.

04 · Findings

What changed on the factory floor?

The findings show sustainability adoption across technology, organization and environment, with measurable operational and economic outcomes.

01

Liquid indigo dyes

The proportion of liquid dyes used in indigo dyeing increased from 40.76% to 94.80% during the observation period.

Liquid indigo required 33–35% less sodium hydrosulfite than conventional granular dyes.
02

Alkali reduction

Sodium hydroxide consumption in fabric dyeing decreased while maintaining fabric quality.

0.0565 → 0.0384 kg/yds · approximately 32.04% reduction
03

Finishing efficiency

The factory adopted STF (Shrink to Fit) technology in denim finishing.

Average steam use reduced by 11.95% over two years.
04

Safety & compliance

Occupational safety measures and other sustainability practices were associated with lower injury rates and the establishment of internal compliance measures.

Injury rate: 8.12 → 5.04 in the finishing & mercerizing department
05

Recycled yarns

The factory began using pre-consumer cotton and post-consumer polyester yarns as raw materials.

Recycled yarns represented 31.4% of total yarn use and increased by 3.74% over two years.
06

Water reuse & cost

Water reuse contributed to lower water consumption and groundwater lifting intensity, with a corresponding reduction in wastewater-treatment cost.

Water intensity: 0.0195 → 0.0158 m³/yds · wastewater-treatment cost saving: 10.99%
05 · Interpretation

What does the study imply?

The paper connects sustainability adoption with organizational performance and the external pressures surrounding export-oriented textile manufacturing.

For theory

The study argues that environmental factors — particularly buyer pressure and compliance requirements — act as dominant external enablers of technological and sustainable innovations in developing-country contexts.

It further proposes broadening the TOE framing by integrating cost-saving and competitive-advantage outcomes as mediating mechanisms linking sustainability adoption with firm performance.

For industry

  • Water reuse can reduce both resource consumption and wastewater-treatment costs.
  • Recycled-fiber utilization can support buyer requirements and market positioning.
  • Sustainability-oriented investments can produce tangible economic benefits alongside environmental outcomes.

Research boundary & future work

The authors note that the two-year observation period may not capture long-term sustainability outcomes. Future research could examine multiple denim manufacturers or other textile sectors, validate the TOE framework more broadly, and incorporate quantitative measures such as cost–benefit analysis and emission-reduction metrics.
